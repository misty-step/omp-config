# Brief Reader Test

Write for an intelligent operator who knows nothing about the situation except
the request. Give that reader the simplest clear understanding of what is
happening, why, and what it means for what comes next.

Make the brief atomic and self-contained. Supply every piece of context the
reader needs; omit everything that does not change understanding or judgment.
Explain mostly why, enough what to support the explanation, and little to no
how unless a mechanism materially changes the choices.

Start with the bottom line, then provide the minimum context that makes it
fully intelligible. Explain material causes, stakes, constraints,
relationships, uncertainty, and consequences in plain language. Follow the
situation rather than a standard template.

When the situation presents a real choice, help the reader understand the live
options and recommend a direction when the evidence supports one. Otherwise,
give the state and its implications without inventing a call.

Before finishing, apply the reader test: could a capable operator understand
the situation and choose the next move from this brief alone? Add missing
context, then remove detail that does not help that test.
